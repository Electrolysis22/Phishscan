PhishScan

Check a link before you trust it

PhishScan looks at the address, the domain and what the page actually does, then explains why it reached its verdict.

1. The link

Checks the structure of the URL for tricks such as IP addresses, look-alike characters, shorteners and brand names in the wrong place.

2. The domain

Looks at how old the domain is, whether its certificate is valid, and whether the link redirects somewhere else.

3. The page

Reads the page itself: where password forms send data, whether it imitates a known brand, hidden frames and disguised code.

Why not just check the web address?

Phishing pages often sit on real, trusted domains, such as hacked websites and free hosting. So a safe-looking address is never enough. PhishScan weighs what the page does, and every point of the score comes with a reason you can read.

About PhishScan

A phishing website detection system that combines machine learning with evidence from the page itself.

How it decides

  • A machine learning model scores the structure of the URL. It contributes at most 25 points.
  • Domain checks look at domain age, certificate validity and redirects.
  • Page checks look at password forms, brand impersonation, hidden iframes and obfuscated code.
  • A page is only called phishing when the score is high and the evidence comes from more than one layer, or from two strong content findings.
  • If the page cannot be downloaded, the result cannot be higher than Suspicious.

What the verdicts mean

Phishing detectedStrong evidence from several checks. Do not enter any information.
SuspiciousSome warning signs, but not enough to be sure. Be careful.
Likely safeNo strong warning signs found. This is not a guarantee.
InconclusiveThe page could not be inspected, so only the link and domain were checked.

Limitations

  • Pages that build their content with JavaScript show less evidence, because only the raw page text is read.
  • Some phishing kits show a harmless page to automated visitors.
  • The scoring weights are hand-tuned and are checked against test data, not proven.
  • A "Likely safe" result lowers risk but never removes it.
